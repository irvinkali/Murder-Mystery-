# Reunion 1989, Props Guide (Spoiler-Safe)

Seven props. Each one will either carry genuinely important information or convincingly appear to. Which is which depends on the solution variant selected at game start, so nothing on this page tells you anything about the plot. Do not add, remove, or substitute props without telling me, since each one is woven into the evidence system.

Three of the seven are **photographs**, and they are the only props that need a little craft work. Nothing is ever printed on the object itself: the app supplies all meaning at the moment a guest finds it, which is what lets the same physical photo mean four different things on four different nights.

Each prop gets a clear NFC sticker (write the URL I provide to it using the free "NFC Tools" app). Props marked [card] instead get a small numbered card, I'll provide a printable sheet styled like the little tented cards on a buffet table.

### A note on the three photographs

Buy a small stack of anonymous thrifted snapshots, most antique malls and thrift stores have a shoebox of them for pennies. You want faces you don't recognise and clothes that look roughly right for the era. Make P5 and P6 from that stack. Don't use real photos of anyone attending; it lands badly, and guests handle these a lot.

When you cut P5, cut **one whole figure out**, not a face, a clean silhouette-shaped hole reads much better across a room than a scribble. Use a craft knife and a ruler for the straight edges.

For P1, the torn-off fourth frame should be genuinely torn and genuinely gone. Don't keep it anywhere in the room.

## Placement Guide

Semi-hidden means visible to someone poking around and invisible to someone standing in the middle of the room. Guests finding these "too early" is fine, the app handles early discoveries gracefully.

Before you place anything, set up two zones the props lean on: a **memory table** (old photos spread out, the yearbook, a bowl of name tags) and a **photo booth corner** (a backdrop, a chair, a curtain or sheet on a rod).

- **P1 (photo strip):** On the floor just under the photo-booth chair, or fallen behind the little table the camera sits on. Somewhere a second copy would have dropped and not been noticed.
- **P2 (yearbook):** On the memory table, in the middle of the stack, so people pick it up all night without ever getting to the end of it.
- **P3 (pill bottle):** Bathroom, medicine cabinet, windowsill, or the bottom of the coat pile. People always snoop in the bathroom.
- **P4 (cassette):** In a box of old junk near the sound gear, or on the windowsill behind the speakers. Somewhere obvious and completely ignorable, because nothing in the room plays tape.
- **P5 (cut-out group photo):** Face up on the memory table with the other photographs, third or fourth from the top. It should get picked up casually and put back at least twice before anyone counts.
- **P6 (photo with writing):** Also on the memory table, face up, ideally weighted under something so nobody flips it by accident early.
- **P7 (key and permit):** On the floor near a side door or fire exit, half under the edge of a chair, like it fell out of a pocket.

## Night-of Notes

- Place props during setup, after the room is dressed. Takes five minutes.
- If a guest picks something up and moves it before the game starts, don't worry, the app ties meaning to the item, not to the location.
- Keep one spare NFC sticker on hand in case one fails a test tap.
- Test-tap each placed prop once during setup. The app shows a neutral pre-game catalogue page, so no spoilers.
- Put the banner up late and badly. It has been in a box for three years and it should look like it.
